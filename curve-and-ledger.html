Where the two subjects actually sit

This is the first thing to get straight, because it changes how the hours get spent.

The UG-AT does not have an Economics section. It tests English Comprehension, Quantitative Ability & Data Interpretation, and Logical Reasoning. Economics earns its place in this programme for three other reasons — it is a scoring board subject, it is what the personal interview will circle around, and it is the degree Bhavin is applying to study. Treat Core Mathematics as the entrance track and Economics as the admission track. They meet in the middle at elasticity, marginal cost, and every chart in the DI set.
Track A · entrance

Core Mathematics

14 modules covering NCERT Class X–XII plus the aptitude layer the school syllabus never teaches: shortcut arithmetic, data interpretation under a clock, and logical reasoning sets. This is the 60% of the composite score. All 14 modules are taught in full under Learn.

Track B · admission

Economics

10 modules from scarcity to monetary policy, ending in a mathematical-economics bridge and an interview module. This is the 40%, the board marks, and the reason the degree will make sense in year one instead of year two. All 10 modules are taught in full under Learn.

The approach, in five moves

Move 01

Build the base before the speed

Months 1–6 are concept-first with untimed practice. Speed drilled onto a shaky base produces confident wrong answers, and this test charges for those.

Move 02

Treat negative marking as a rule, not a risk

Every drill in the Solved bank is scored with a penalty. The habit to build is skipping cleanly — a skipped question costs nothing, a guessed one costs real marks.

Move 03

Forecast topics, don't predict questions

The Forecast section weights topics by how consistently they appear across the UG-AT, its official mock, and the neighbouring IPMAT/JIPMAT papers. It tells you where to spend hours — and then reconstructs the question shapes those hours should be spent on.

Move 04

Log every error by cause

Concept gap, calculation slip, misread stem, or time panic. Four causes, four different fixes. Most students re-solve the question and learn nothing.

Move 05

Prepare the interview from month one

Forty percent of the composite score is a conversation. One economics article a week, summarised in six lines, for sixteen months, is what makes that conversation easy.

Shared with ISI / CMI

The overlap is large — use it

Algebra, sequences, functions, counting, probability and coordinate geometry serve ISI, CMI and the UG-AT at once. The UG-AT-only additions are DI, LR and English. Roughly 70% of the maths hours are shared.

The test, as currently known

The UG-AT ran for the first time in December 2025 for the 2026–30 batch. One edition means the pattern is documented but not yet stable. Everything below should be re-verified against the official brochure when applications open.

60 multiple-choice questions · 120 minutes · computer-based · sectional cut-offs reported in the first edition · syllabus drawn from NCERT Classes X–XII.

One genuine open question: the marking scheme. Most coverage of the 2025 edition reports +1 for a correct answer and −1/3 for a wrong one. At least one exam-analysis site reported +3 and −1. Those are the same ratio, so strategy is unaffected — but confirm the absolute numbers from the official mock before the exam, because they change what a sectional cut-off means in raw marks.

What the first edition felt like

Quant & DI

Moderate to hard

The toughest of the three, and the section that decided ranks. Notably lighter on Class 12 topics than expected — more aptitude arithmetic and data interpretation than calculus and matrices.

Reasoning

Easier, but odd

High-scoring and methodical rather than tricky, with an unusually heavy run of odd-one-out and alphabet-based questions. This is the section where late preparation still moves the needle.

English

Moderate

Dense reading passages and grammar that rewards rules over instinct. Over-attempting here is the classic way to bleed marks; 12 attempts with 10 right beats 15 with 10 right.

Eligibility, in plain terms

  • Class 12 passed, with Mathematics as a Class 12 subject — non-negotiable, and it is why the subject choice matters now.
  • At least 60% in Class 10 Mathematics.
  • Under 20 years of age as of the 1 August of the joining year.
  • Qualify the UG-AT, then a personal interview. Final call: 60% test, 40% interview.
  • Four-year residential degree at the Jigani campus, with an exit option after three years. B.Sc. (Hons) Economics carries minors in data science and business studies.

What a forecast can and cannot do. Only one UG-AT has ever been held, so this rests on one paper plus its official mock plus adjacent exams. It cannot tell you the questions. What it can tell you — reliably — is the shapes that keep returning: mark-up with successive discount, repeated replacement in a mixture, multiply-then-add series, alphabet positions, elasticity read off a straight demand curve. Those templates recur because they test cleanly in ninety seconds. Learn the template and the specific numbers stop mattering.

Error review is the smallest block and the one most often skipped. It should never be the block that gets cut — re-solving a wrong question is worth more than three new ones.

The error log — four causes, four fixes

Cause A

Concept gap

You did not know the rule. Fix: back to the module, re-derive it, then five fresh questions on that rule alone. Do not move on the same day.

Cause B

Calculation slip

You knew it and dropped a sign or a decimal. Fix: mental-arithmetic drill, ten minutes daily, and the habit of estimating the answer's magnitude before computing it.

Cause C

Misread stem

You answered a question that was not asked — "at least" read as "exactly", percentage of the wrong base. Fix: underline the quantity being asked for before starting. Every time.

Cause D

Time panic

Correct method, ran out of clock, guessed. Fix: this is a selection problem, not a speed problem. Practise the two-pass sweep until skipping stops feeling like losing.

Solved bank

A full bank at UG-AT shape and difficulty — filter by section or by level. Every question carries the full working, and most carry the trap they were built around. Scoring uses the reported penalty: +1 correct, −1/3 wrong, 0 for skipped. Skipping is a legitimate move here, as it is in the hall.

The helping document

The things worth having on one page in the last month.

Percentage ↔ fraction

  • 1/6 = 16.67% · 1/7 ≈ 14.29% · 1/8 = 12.5%
  • 1/9 = 11.11% · 1/11 ≈ 9.09% · 1/12 ≈ 8.33%
  • Successive change: a + b + ab/100
  • Up x% then down x% is always a net loss of x²/100%

Averages & alligation

  • Alligation ratio = (H − m) : (m − L)
  • Average speed for equal distances = 2ab/(a+b)
  • Replacing one item: new avg = old avg + (change ÷ n)

Algebra

  • a³+b³+c³−3abc = (a+b+c)(a²+b²+c²−ab−bc−ca)
  • Roots: sum −b/a, product c/a, discriminant b²−4ac
  • AM ≥ GM ≥ HM, equality only when all terms are equal
  • x + 1/x ≥ 2 for positive x

Progressions

  • AP sum = n/2 · (2a + (n−1)d)
  • GP sum = a(rⁿ−1)/(r−1), infinite = a/(1−r) for |r|<1
  • Σn = n(n+1)/2 · Σn² = n(n+1)(2n+1)/6

Counting

  • Circular arrangements of n: (n−1)!
  • At least one: total − none
  • Distributing n identical into r distinct: C(n+r−1, r−1)
  • Letters with repeats: n! / (p! q! …)

Geometry

  • Triples worth knowing: 3-4-5, 5-12-13, 7-24-25, 8-15-17
  • Equilateral area = (√3/4)a²
  • Similar figures: areas scale as k², volumes as k³
  • Line through (x₁,y₁),(x₂,y₂): slope (y₂−y₁)/(x₂−x₁)

Econ ↔ maths bridge

  • Price elasticity = (dQ/dP)·(P/Q) — a derivative, scaled
  • Marginal cost = derivative of total cost
  • Profit is maximised where MR = MC and MC is rising
  • A budget line is P₁x₁ + P₂x₂ = M — slope −P₁/P₂

DI speed habits

  • Read the units and the footnote before the numbers
  • Approximate first; only compute exactly if the options are close
  • Percentage change ≈ difference ÷ smaller, for changes under 15%
  • If two questions in a set need the same sub-total, compute it once

Exam-day protocol

Pass 1 · 55 min

Sweep for certainty

Answer only what you can see the route to within about 20 seconds. Flag everything else and move. Expect to answer roughly 35 questions here.

Pass 2 · 45 min

Work the flagged set

Return to flagged questions in ascending order of effort, not paper order. Complete DI sets whose table you have already read — the reading cost is already paid.

Pass 3 · 15 min

Verify, don't gamble

Re-check the ten answers you were least sure of. Guess only where you have eliminated two options — below that, the penalty makes guessing negative in expectation.

Last 5 min

Stop adding

Confirm nothing is left half-marked and every intended answer is registered. No new attempts. This is the discipline that separates a good mock score from a good real score.

Interview preparation

Expect the "why" chain

Why economics, why an IIM at 17 rather than at 22, why this over a B.Sc. at ISI or a BA at a Delhi college. Each answer should survive one follow-up question. Prepare the follow-up, not the answer.

Know your own file

Anything on the form is fair game — a subject, a project, an olympiad, a certificate. If you cannot discuss it for two minutes, either learn it properly or leave it off.

Have three live numbers

Current inflation, current repo rate, latest GDP growth — with the month they refer to. Not to show off, but because someone who tracks them talks about the economy differently.

"I don't know" is an allowed move

Said once, cleanly, followed by how you would find out, it reads as maturity. Said while bluffing, it reads as the opposite. Panels are extremely good at telling the two apart.
